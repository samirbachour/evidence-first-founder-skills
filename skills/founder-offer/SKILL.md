# Offer architecture grounded in what exists

Read the exact SKU or service description, verified policy, owner-provided product list, real competitor feedback, and financial unit costs. Use Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers* only as a conceptual framework, never celebrity endorsement or testimony.

Identify (a) the actual core promise, (b) doubts supported by real evidence, (c) candidate low-cost ways to reduce friction, and (d) the cost of delivering each added item. For physical products, inspect print quality, sizing, production, shipping, refunds and customer support before changing a guarantee. For services, define included hours, delivery, scope and follow-up from actual published commitments. For apps, specify which capabilities exist versus proposals.

For existing brands, preserve established positioning, product naming and voice unless the owner asks for a change. Avoid claims that a product guarantees social, financial, medical or other personal outcomes without substantiation. Do not publish private fulfillment infrastructure in consumer-facing copy except where required for accuracy or disclosure.

Any offer revision must include `change, source of objection, real delivery requirements, additional unit cost, proof/unknowns, test`. Do not alter a live listing or refund policy without separate authorization. Output: `founder/offer.md` and an optional approved draft; send cost changes to `founder-cfo`.
